# A small guide to candidate readings

This is a curated public selection adapted from the project's category and boundary material, not a complete taxonomy, rule engine or publication of its response templates. These are questions to investigate in context, not requirements every story must satisfy.

| Area | Evidence worth inspecting | Where the diagnosis can go wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Causal and physical continuity | The established incoming state, a consequential event and the later passage that depends on it. Identify the missing or contradictory connection. | Ellipsis can omit an unimportant step. A nonlinear or impossible world may have its own supported rules; unfamiliarity alone is not contradiction. |
| Scene function and pacing | What a scene sustains or changes in attention, understanding, pressure or relationship; compare the actual beginning and end. | A pause, ritual or atmosphere can be the function. No plot movement does not establish wasted space. |
| Relationship signaling | Differences in what people notice, risk or want from one another; distinguish their positions rather than assuming symmetry. | Unequal attachment or delayed recognition may be the intended effect. Lack of reconciliation is not automatically a failed turn. |
| Information and orientation | The specific fact the reader needs now, what the passage supplies, and what the withheld information makes possible. | Mystery, productive disorientation and a promise of later explanation are not interchangeable. Do not demand a reveal the work never promised. |
| Worldbuilding in use | A rule's effect on access, choices, cost or expectations where the scene calls for it. | An archive, handbook or setting note need not dramatize every fact. Volume of lore alone does not establish the problem. |
| Draft layers | Version labels, author notes and the status of apparently conflicting passages. | A visibly provisional alternative is not adopted canon. Mixed drafts require version questions before polished repairs. |

## Keep these judgments separate

**Symptom:** What can be pointed to in the supplied text? "The same object is upstairs and at the desk" is more inspectable than "the scene is weak."

**Possible cause:** Why might that effect have arisen? A missed revision is a hypothesis unless editing evidence establishes it. Several causes can fit one symptom.

**Severity:** How consequential is the issue for this scene or brief? A local wording defect need not invalidate the plot. The impact may be unknown until dependent passages are available.

**Confidence:** How strongly does the inspected evidence support this reading? Do not use the intensity of a reaction as proof of the inferred cause. Qualitative confidence labels are not calibrated probabilities.

**Intent:** What did the author declare, and what did the reviewer infer? Deliberate design matters, but saying an effect is intended does not prove it reaches readers. Conversely, a reviewer's preference does not invalidate it.

**Author preference and decision:** An author can accept, reject or adapt a suggestion. Agreement establishes a revision decision, not objective proof that the original was defective or that every reader will prefer the result.

## A compact review note

For a consequential candidate, identify the passage and draft scope, the evidence, the reading it supports, a plausible alternative, and the smallest useful next question or optional revision. Explain what the change would gain and what it could lose.

Notice a strength when it materially affects the diagnosis or what should be preserved. There is no required praise quota, score balance or obligation to invent a fault. The right result can be a specific repair, no repair, or an unresolved question.

The [examples](WORKED-EXAMPLES.md) use this approach without claiming a validated machine schema. Consult [evidence and limitations](EVIDENCE.md) before treating documentary categories as tested capabilities.
