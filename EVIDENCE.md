# Evidence and limitations

Edition: 2026-10-26. This is a source-informed documentary edition. No model experiment, product test or literary benchmark was run for it.

## What exists in the inspected source

The selected ten documents include a project context, a category guide, a first issue catalog, an initial candidate schema, conventions and an addendum, later issue expansions and boundary examples, response templates, and a risk review. Together they describe evidence-linked critique with room for intent, uncertainty, strengths and alternative revisions.

The catalog is labeled v1 in its filename; the initial schema is a draft; the selected expansion and boundary filenames carry v3; the response and risk-review filenames carry v4. Several internal version labels differ from their filenames. These are versioned design materials, not evidence of one consolidated accepted runtime release.

All ten selected files matched the corresponding nested laptop copy byte-for-byte at this review. That establishes identity of those files only, not complete archive parity, absence of later work elsewhere or independent validation of their contents.

## Counterevidence matters

The v4 risk review explicitly leaves identifier normalization unresolved and describes boundary conditions as class-local rather than schema-enforced. It warns that intent and strengths can be underweighted, symptoms mistaken for causes, and distinct questions flattened into broad labels such as pacing or clarity.

The response-template material includes places to state false-positive risks and boundaries. Having those fields does not establish that every use fills them, that any validator enforces them, or that the resulting judgment is sound. The public guide is an authored treatment of these concerns, not a repair of the source schema.

Some source language is stronger than this edition adopts, including treating author confirmation as an issue status and asking what small change would prove a diagnosis. Here, author agreement is a decision and a proposed revision is an editorial comparison, not objective proof of defect. The original materials remain unchanged.

## What was not tested

Files named boundary tests contain written examples and conditions for distinguishing issue classes. Their existence is not a test-execution receipt. This review establishes no accuracy rate, reader study, reliable automated literary judgment or measured superiority to ordinary editing.

The three [public examples](WORKED-EXAMPLES.md) and annotations are newly authored illustrations. They neither validate the corpus nor show that a deployed application produced the readings. The [issue guide](ISSUE-GUIDE.md) selects a few useful areas; it is not a wholesale export of the corpus or its private prompting material.

Remaining questions include consistency across genres and draft states, whether reviewers preserve meaningful ambiguity, how well claims remain tied to evidence, and whether proposed revisions improve the author's intended effect. These are research and editorial questions, not completed results or a new experiment authorized by this edition.
